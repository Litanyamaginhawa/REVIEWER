Wellness

Defined wellness is the optimal health and vitality encompassing the dimensions of well-being. Wellness is relatively new concept that expand our idea of health and refers to living life to its fullest.

The Eight Dimension of Wellness

1. Occupational Wellness

  • Ability to finding fulfillment in one's job; enjoyment in work.
  • Gain personal satisfaction and to find enrichment in one's life through work.
  • Feels a connection with the people in the workplace, and has opportunities to learn and be challenged.

2. Financial Wellness

  • Ability to spend no more than the limit of income.
  • Save money for emergency needs.

3. Environmental Wellness

  • Ability to take care of the environment.
  • Protecting one's self against environmental hazards.
  • Contributing to make the world clean and safe place.

4. Intellectual Wellness

Refers to active participation in scholastic, cultural, and community activities. It is important to gain and maintain intellectual wellness because it expands knowledge and skills in order to live a stimulating, successful life. Focuses on how you think.

5. Spiritual Wellness

  • Ability to establish a value system and act on a system of beliefs, faith, values, ethics, principles and morals.
  • Having a guiding principle and philosophy.
  • Find meaning and purpose in one's existence.

6. Social Wellness

  • Ability to interact, develop and maintain supportive and satisfying relationship.
  • Positive interaction to other individual.
  • Participating in, and successfully contributing to the needs of the community.

7. Emotional Wellness

Inspires self-care, relaxation, stress reduction and the development of inner strength. It is important to be attentive to both positive and negative feelings and be able to understand how to handle these emotions.

8. Physical Wellness

Is the ability to maintain a healthy quality of life that allows us to get the most out of our daily activities without undue fatigue or physical stress. Focuses on your physical body.

Physical Fitness

  • State of health and well being
  • Specifically the ability to perform aspects of sports
  • Achieved to proper nutrition
  • Moderate physical exercise
  • Sufficient rest

Hypokinetic Disease

Condition occur as a result of lack of exercise and movement.

11 Components of Physical Fitness

Health Related Components

Cardiovascular Endurance

Ability of the circulatory and respiratory system to supply oxygen during physical activity.

Muscular Strength

It is the amount of force a muscle can produce.

Muscular Endurance

It is the ability of the muscle to perform continuous without fatiguing.

Flexibility

Ability of the muscles and joints to extend themselves through their range of motion.

Body Composition

Percent of body weight that is fat compared to other body tissues. (Fat - Skin fold caliper) BMI – Body Mass Index

Skill Related Components

Agility

Ability to change position quickly.

Coordination

It's the ability to use the sense of sight and thought together.

Reaction time

Is the time taken for a person to respond into a stimulus.

Speed

Ability to perform a movement into a short period of time.

Power

Ability to do strength performance.

Balance

Maintain or regrain the equilibrium while moving or stationary.

Circuit Training Exercise

  • Consists of a consecutive series of timed exercises performed one after the other, with varying amounts of rest between each exercise.
  • Circuit Training is an excellent way to simultaneously improve mobility, strength, and stamina.
  • Circuit Training is one of the best methods of exercising because it provides excellent all-around fitness, such as tone, strength, and weight reduction. In short, maximum results in minimum time.
  • An example of simple circuit training workout might consist of Push-ups, Sit-ups, Squats, Lunges and High Knees.

Types of Circuit Training

1. Timed circuit

  • This type of circuit involves working to a set time period for both rest and exercise intervals.
  • A typical timed circuit is 30 seconds of exercise and 30 seconds rest.
  • While a timed circuit exercise for athlete is 80 seconds of exercise and 20 seconds rest.

2. Competition Circuit

This is similar to a timed circuit but you push yourself to see how many repetitions you can do. For example: You may be able to do complete 12 push-ups in 30 seconds. The idea is to keep the time period the same, but try to increase the number of repetitions you can do in the set time period.

Repetition Circuit

The idea is that the Fittest group may do, let's say 20 repetitions of each exercise, the intermediate group might only do 15 repetitions, while the beginners might only do 10 repetitions of each exercise.

3 Types of Exercise

1. Warm Up Exercise

A period or act of preparation for a game, performance, or exercise session, involving gentle exercise.

2. Work Out Exercise

A practice or exercise to test or improve one's fitness for athletic competition, ability or performance.

3. Cool Down Exercise

To allow the body to gradually transition to a resting or near-resting state after an intense exercise.

3 Types of Stretching

1. Static Stretching

The most common type of stretching, static stretching, is executed by extending the targeted muscle group to its maximal point and holding it for 16 seconds or more.

2. Dynamic Stretching

Dynamic stretches are movements performed at a slower pace than most workouts. People tend to do dynamic stretches to warm up their muscles and prepare for exercise. Dynamic stretching involves making active movements that stretch the muscles to their full range of motion.

3. Ballistic Stretching

This type of stretching is typically used for athletic drills and utilizes repeated bouncing movement to stretch the targeted muscle group. While these bouncing movements usually trigger the stretch reflex and may cause increased risk for injury, they can be safely performed if done from low-velocity to high-velocity and preceded by static stretching.

F.I.T.T. Principle

F.I.T.T. stands for Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type — the four factors applied across Cardiovascular Endurance, Muscular Endurance, Muscular Strength, and Flexibility.

Frequency

  • Cardiovascular Endurance: Exercise 3-5 times per week
  • Muscular Endurance: Exercises 2-4 times per week
  • Muscular Strength: Weight train 2-4 times per week
  • Flexibility: Daily stretching

Intensity

  • Cardiovascular Endurance: Train at 60-80% of target heart rate
  • Muscular Endurance: Add or maintain weight and repetitions
  • Muscular Strength: 60-75% of max, 3 sets of 8-12 repetitions
  • Flexibility: Stretch muscles and hold beyond its normal length

Time

  • Cardiovascular Endurance: 20-60 minutes per session
  • Muscular Endurance: About 30-60 minutes
  • Muscular Strength: About 30-60 minutes
  • Flexibility: Hold each stretch 10-15 seconds

Type

  • Cardiovascular Endurance: Any aerobic activity keeping the heart rate within the target zone
  • Muscular Endurance: Resistance training yoga, Pilates, light weights
  • Muscular Strength: Anaerobic activities such as weight lifting and sit ups
  • Flexibility: Stretches that allow the body to move through the full range of motion
